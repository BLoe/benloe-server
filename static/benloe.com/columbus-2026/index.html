“Columbus” Auction League · 2026

The Draft Recap

Twelve managers. $2,400. 192 players. And a room that flatly refused to pay for quarterbacks.

Everybody spent nearly everything — $2,393 of $2,400, with only three teams leaving so much as a dollar on the table. Jahmyr Gibbs was the priciest man in the room at $68. The cheapest starting quarterback cost $1. Four different teams paid that.

The quarterback thing

This league awards six points per passing touchdown, which is supposed to make quarterbacks expensive. Reader, it did not.

Patrick Mahomes went for $1. Trevor Lawrence, $1. Jared Goff, Matthew Stafford, Jordan Love, Kyler Murray, Brock Purdy — all $1. Dak Prescott cost $3. Justin Herbert, $4. Nine of the twelve teams walked away with a starting quarterback for eleven dollars or less, and several of them are now sitting on a backup who projects within ten points of their starter.

Josh Allen cost $34, and he may well be worth it. But the gap between him and a dollar bin full of 330-point arms is the single loudest thing in this draft — and the reason so many rosters have $30 more receiver than they probably expected.

Team by team

01 Threat Level Midnight

Bought the same quarterback twice and still won the draft.

Spent$200
Starters1653
Priciest$49
vs board+22

The most sensible draft in the room, and it is not especially close. Nobody on this roster cost more than $49, the money is split almost exactly down the middle between running backs ($91) and receivers ($93), and the result is the highest projected lineup in the league. This is what happens when you refuse to fall in love with anybody.

The comedy is at quarterback. They took Caleb Williams for $5, then took Trevor Lawrence for $1 — two passers projecting within two points of each other. Six dollars for a starter and an identical twin who has to watch from the bench. Colston Loveland at $9 against a $17 board price was the tidiest buy on the roster.

Verdict: boring, disciplined, first place. Deeply annoying.

02 The Empire Strikes Dak

Two elite quarterbacks for twelve dollars, and a very expensive backfield.

Spent$198
Starters1601
RB spend$113
vs board−25

The quarterback room is the quiet coup of the entire draft. Lamar Jackson at $11 was one of the best buys of the night against a $21 board price, and then Patrick Mahomes arrived for a dollar — a genuine top-five arm who will spend most Sundays on the bench. Twelve dollars for that pair, in a six-point-passing-touchdown league, is close to daylight robbery.

The backfield is where the money went and where the risk lives. Jonathan Taylor at $66 was the second-priciest player in the draft and about ten over the board; the Josh Jacobs buy at $15 was the single largest overpay in the room against pre-draft prices. $113 in running backs is a lot of eggs in a basket that gets tackled for a living — but this is still a top-three projected lineup, so the eggs are good ones.

Verdict: paid up at the position everyone else got for free, and got the cheapest quarterbacks in the league anyway. Hard to argue with.

The most concentrated roster in the league by a wide margin. Derrick Henry ($53), Christian McCaffrey ($53) and De'Von Achane ($44) account for $150 of the $200. That left $43 for four starting receivers and ten players at a dollar or two.

When it works it is glorious. The bench, however, is the second-thinnest in the league at 387 projected points, which means the injury math is unforgiving: this is a roster with no answers, only starters. Brock Purdy at $1 against an $11 board price was the steal of their night.

Verdict: a beautiful, terrifying machine. Two hamstrings from a rebuild.

04 TylerMcNutt

The only manager who looked at tight end and said “yes, both.”

Spent$200
Starters1601
TE spend$41
vs board−4

Brock Bowers at $33 and Tyler Warren at $8 — $41 at tight end, nearly triple the league average, and the only roster in the draft that starts two of them by playing one in the flex. It is a real strategy: in a league where the median starting tight end is worth about a hundred points, having two good ones is a weekly edge over half the room.

It is paid for with a genuinely lean backfield ($61, no back over $31) and Puka Nacua at $51 holding the receiving corps together. The bench is 392 points, third-thinnest. Everything has to stay upright.

Verdict: the most distinctive roster in the league. If the tight ends hit, nobody else has an answer.

The best value draft in the league, and it isn't close. Plus 37 against the board, with the single biggest bargain of the night — David Montgomery at $18 against a $34 price — and Mike Evans for $16 against $30.

The shape is extreme: $162 of $200 on eight wide receivers, in a league that starts four plus a flex. Nobody on the roster cost more than Nico Collins at $32. The quarterback and the tight end cost a dollar each, combined, and it barely matters because the receiver room is deep enough to absorb a bye week without blinking.

Verdict: won the auction on points-per-dollar. Fifth on projection only because the running backs are held together with tape.

Spent$196

Jahmyr Gibbs at $68 was the top price of the auction, and paired with Jeremiyah Love at $39 it makes $129 of running back — the second-heaviest backfield in the league. The receivers that money left behind are thin: Tetairoa McMillan at $29 and then a very sharp drop.

The bench tells its own story at 832 points, second-highest in the league, most of it Justin Herbert sitting behind Jalen Hurts. They also left $4 unspent, which in an auction where $1 buys a 330-point quarterback is four dollars with somewhere better to be.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba ($48), A.J. Brown ($30) and Tee Higgins ($28) form the highest projecting receiver group in the league — $144 of the $200, and worth it. Verdict: elite at the position this league rewards most, and one Kyren Williams hamstring from a very long autumn.

The second-most efficient draft in the league by price — nothing on this roster was more than four dollars over its board value, and Bijan Robinson at $64 came in almost exactly at market. Sam LaPorta for $4 and Jadarian Price for $10 were both proper steals.

And then there is the bench: 866 projected points, the most in the league by 34, sat in a roster that starts four receivers and one flex. Nine wideouts is a lot of wideouts. Some of that is real insurance and some of it is value that simply cannot get on the field — there is probably a trade in here that turns two of them into one starter somewhere.

Verdict: bought beautifully, hoarded slightly. The best skill-position starting five in the league is in there somewhere.

Only three players on this roster cost a dollar or two — by far the fewest in the league, where the average team has seven. Nobody cost more than Trey McBride at $34. Paid up for the best quarterback in football, then drafted a defensive back.

Spent$200
Starters1502

Josh Allen ($34), James Cook ($60) and Omarion Hampton ($47) is $147 on three players, and all three are good. Allen is the highest-projecting quarterback in the league by a distance and in a six-point-passing-TD format that is a real, permanent weekly edge.

The problem is arithmetic. That left $43 for everything else, and this roster carries exactly four wide receivers in a league that starts four. Two of them cost a dollar. There is no flex option, no bye-week cover, and no margin — and, in a detail we are legally required to report, one of the sixteen roster spots went to a DB, in a league with nowhere to play one.

Verdict: the best single player in the draft and the least room to manoeuvre. Everything rests on Allen being as good as he costs.

Superlatives

Steal of the draft. David Montgomery, $18 against a $34 board — Super Nintendo Chalmers. Honourable mention to Mike Evans at $16 and Brock Purdy at $1.
Most expensive man in the room. Jahmyr Gibbs, $68, The DC Lumber Company. Jonathan Taylor was next at $66.
Best bench player nobody can start. Patrick Mahomes, $1, sitting behind Lamar Jackson on The Empire Strikes Dak. Trevor Lawrence at $1 on Threat Level Midnight is right there with him.
Most committed to a bit. Super Nintendo Chalmers, who put $162 of $200 into wide receivers and then watched it work.
Best dollar anybody spent. Brock Purdy, $1, Washington Fantasy Team. He projects 350 points against Josh Allen’s 406 — so the most expensive quarterback in the draft bought 56 extra points for $33.

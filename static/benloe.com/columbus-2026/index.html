“Columbus” Auction League · 2026

The Draft Recap

Twelve managers. $2,400. 192 players. And a room that flatly refused to pay for quarterbacks.

Everybody spent nearly everything — $2,393 of $2,400, with only three teams leaving so much as a dollar on the table. Jahmyr Gibbs was the priciest man in the room at $68. The cheapest starting quarterback cost $1. Four different teams paid that.

The quarterback thing

This league pays six points a passing touchdown, which everyone assumes should make quarterbacks expensive. It doesn't, and this draft is a clean demonstration of why: six points a throw lifts every quarterback at once. It does not create scarcity, and scarcity is the only thing an auction actually prices.

Twelve teams need one starter each. There are comfortably twenty men who will throw for 300 points this year. So the twentieth-best quarterback is nearly as good as the twelfth, the replacement bar sits somewhere absurd, and the market correctly decided that almost nobody is worth paying for.

Correctly, mostly. Josh Allen went for $34 and projects about 55 points clear of the field, which is roughly three points a week — a real edge, and arguably worth it. Everyone else got the position solved for pocket money: Lamar Jackson $11, Joe Burrow $19, Drake Maye $11, Dak Prescott $3, Brock Purdy $1. Seven teams built a two-quarterback room for under $15 total, which buys bye-week cover, injury insurance and the freedom to play matchups every week. That is not a wasted roster spot; in this format it is close to the best dollar on the board.

Superlatives

The opening run. The first four players nominated were all running backs — Bijan Robinson ($64), Jahmyr Gibbs ($68), Jonathan Taylor ($66) and Christian McCaffrey ($53). $251 left the room in four nominations, before anybody had drafted a receiver.
Nearly half this draft cost pocket change. Sixty-one players went for $1 and another twenty for $2 — 81 of 192 roster spots, or 42% of the league, filled for a combined $101.
Steal of the draft. David Montgomery, $18 against a $34 board — Super Nintendo Chalmers. Honourable mention to Mike Evans at $16 and Brock Purdy at $1.
Biggest overpay, joint. Josh Jacobs at $15 against a $1 board (The Empire Strikes Dak) and DJ Moore at $23 against $9 (Washington Fantasy Team). Both fourteen dollars clear of what this room usually pays.
Most expensive man in the room. Jahmyr Gibbs, $68, The DC Lumber Company. Jonathan Taylor was next at $66.
Best dollar anybody spent. Brock Purdy, $1, Washington Fantasy Team. He projects 350 points against Josh Allen’s 406 — so the most expensive quarterback in the draft bought 56 extra points for $33.
Best insurance policy in the league. Patrick Mahomes, $1, behind Lamar Jackson on The Empire Strikes Dak. Two top-shelf quarterbacks, twelve dollars, byes in different weeks — and if Jackson stays healthy, Mahomes is the most tradeable dollar asset in the room.
Week 11 is going to hurt somebody. Everything’s Fine has five starters on bye that week — Stafford, Kyren Williams, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, A.J. Brown and Harold Fannin. That is most of a starting lineup, and it is on the calendar right now.
Best correlation play. IslandExclusives took Joe Burrow at $19 and Ja'Marr Chase at $55. When that connection hits for three touchdowns, it wins the week twice over.
Best bench in the league. Closed For Repairs is sitting on J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle. Any one of them would start at running back for half this room, and none of them will start here until somebody gets hurt — at which point this team will not even blink.
Most committed to a bit. Super Nintendo Chalmers, who put $162 of $200 into wide receivers and then watched it work.
Most expensive four dollars nobody spent. The DC Lumber Company finished on $196. In an auction where a dollar was buying 330-point quarterbacks, that is real money left in the wallet.
Last man off the board. Joe Mixon, $1, pick 192, to Pleasantly Pathetic — who also took pick 190. A quiet, dignified end to proceedings.

Team by team

“The board” below means Gavel’s pre-draft price for a player, calculated from this league’s own 2024 and 2025 auction results — so a bargain here is a bargain against what this room actually pays, not against a magazine.

East Village All-Stars

Won every bid and forgot to buy a second running back.

PriciestBijan $64
WRs / RBs9 / 4
Best buyLaPorta $4
Worst byeW10, 4 out

Nominated Bijan Robinson first, paid $64, got him at almost exactly fair price, and then never overpaid again — not one player on this roster went more than four dollars above the board. Sam LaPorta for $4 is the kind of buy that wins a season quietly; Jadarian Price for $10 is the kind you bring up in March.

The shape is the problem, and it is not the nine receivers — in a four-WR league with a flex, receiver depth is the correct thing to hoard, and it covers the Week 10 bye that takes out four starters at once. It is the four running backs. Behind Bijan there is D'Andre Swift and a lot of optimism, no handcuff anywhere, and if Bijan misses time this lineup has nothing to put in the hole. Three of those receivers are worth more to a running-back-poor rival than they are to this bench.

Verdict: the best starting eleven in the league and a genuinely lopsided roster. Trade receivers for a back before somebody needs one.

Threat Level Midnight

Spent nothing at quarterback and got the Bears stack for six dollars.

PriciestSaquon $49
RB / WR split$91 / $93
QB room$6 total
StackWilliams → Loveland

The most level-headed draft in the room. Nobody cost more than Saquon Barkley at $49, the money split almost exactly half to backs and half to receivers, and there is no hole anywhere in the lineup. Breece Hall, Drake London, Ladd McConkey, Brian Thomas — none of them a headline, all of them starters. Colston Loveland at $9 was outright theft at tight end.

The quarterback room reads like an accident and works like a plan: Caleb Williams for $5 and Trevor Lawrence for $1, byes in different weeks, and a matchup to pick every Sunday. Williams also throws to Loveland, so the cheapest tight end buy of the draft doubles as a stack — when Chicago has a big afternoon, this roster gets paid twice.

Verdict: no weakness, no drama, and a correlated cherry on top. Extremely irritating.

Washington Fantasy Team

Three bell-cow backs, and actually bought the handcuff.

On RBs$153
On WRs$43
HandcuffBlack behind CMC
Best buyPurdy $1

Derrick Henry, Christian McCaffrey and De'Von Achane — $150 of $200 on three men who all need volume. It is a real strategy in a league where running back is the scarce thing, and on the right Sunday it buries people.

The tell that this was deliberate rather than reckless: they also took Kaelon Black, the back behind McCaffrey in San Francisco. That is the only proper handcuff on this roster and the one that matters most. What it does not solve is the receiving corps — $43 for four starting spots, the fourth currently Ryan Flournoy, taken with the second-to-last pick of the draft. In a league that starts four, that is where the season gets decided.

Verdict: the backfield is frightening and correctly insured. The receivers need work, and there is no money left to do it with.

Ten of sixteen players cost a dollar or two, and the starting lineup is still excellent: Justin Jefferson, Zay Flowers, Garrett Wilson and Terry McLaurin, with Kenneth Walker at $40 — four under the board. This is what patience buys in an auction where everybody else spends early.

It comes with a real Minnesota stack, Kyler Murray throwing to Jefferson, which is the sort of thing that turns a good week into a blowout. And they took Brashard Smith behind Walker in Kansas City, so the one expensive back is covered. The exposure is Week 6, when Murray, Jefferson and A.J. Brown are all out together, and the bench behind them is thin enough that the answer will have to come off waivers.

Verdict: far better than the price tags suggest, and better prepared than it looks. Circle Week 6.

TylerMcNutt

Two starting tight ends, both on bye in Week 13.

On TEs$41
PriciestNacua $51
HandcuffJudkins / Sampson
Worst byeW13, 4 out

Brock Bowers for $33 and Tyler Warren for $8 — the biggest tight end bill in a room where seven of twelve teams spent five dollars or less there, and the only roster playing two of them by pushing one into the flex. It is a genuine weekly edge: most of this league starts somebody who scores about a hundred, and this team beats that twice.

The catch is written on the calendar. Bowers and Warren share a Week 13 bye, and so do Josh Downs and Jalen Nailor — four starters out at once, including the entire strategy. There is a Cleveland handcuff in Quinshon Judkins and Dylan Sampson, which is smart, but the flex is doing two jobs and one Sunday a year it will not be able to do either.

Verdict: the most distinctive roster in the draft, with a landmine already visible in the schedule.

IslandExclusives

Bought the Bengals passing game and a spare starting quarterback.

PriciestChase $55
StackBurrow → Chase
QB room$22 total
ReachJeanty, $12 over

Joe Burrow at $19 and Ja'Marr Chase at $55 is the best correlation play in the draft — when Cincinnati puts up thirty, this roster collects on both ends of it. Then Dak Prescott for $3 as the second quarterback, which is bye cover, injury insurance and a trade chip in one roster spot.

Ashton Jeanty at $49 was twelve dollars over the board and a lot of faith in a rookie, though they did back it up by taking Mike Washington behind him in Las Vegas. George Kittle at $2 and DK Metcalf at $3 are the buys that pay for the enthusiasm, and Mark Andrews on the bench is real tight end insurance in a league where most teams have exactly one.

Verdict: the sharpest structural draft here — stacked, insured, and cheap everywhere it could afford to be.

The Empire Strikes Dak

Twelve dollars for Lamar Jackson and Patrick Mahomes.

PriciestTaylor $66
QB room$12 total
On RBs$113
ReachJacobs, $14 over

Lamar Jackson at $11 was one of the best buys of the night, and then Patrick Mahomes arrived for a dollar. That is not a wasted bench spot — it is two quarterbacks with byes in different weeks, a matchup play every Sunday, and by October it is the most tradeable dollar in this league to whichever manager loses their starter.

The money instead went into the backfield: $113, led by Jonathan Taylor at $66, ten over the board and the second-priciest player of the night. The Josh Jacobs bid at $15 against a board price of one was the most enthusiastic moment of anybody's evening. No handcuff behind Taylor, which for that money is the one thing missing.

Verdict: won the quarterback market outright and paid retail everywhere else. Go and insure Taylor.

The DC Lumber Company

Paid the top price of the draft and built a bench most teams would start.

PriciestGibbs $68
Unspent$4
Bench RBsBrooks, Corum
Byesnever 3 out

Jahmyr Gibbs at $68 was the biggest number of the night and Jeremiyah Love at $39 went next to it, which is a lot of running back and shows in a receiving corps that drops off sharply after Tetairoa McMillan.

What makes it work is the rest of the roster. Jonathon Brooks and Blake Corum are both sitting on this bench and both would start for several teams here — this is one of very few rosters that can lose a starting back and shrug. The byes are beautifully spread, never more than two starters out in any week, which is the least glamorous good thing you can say about a draft and one of the most useful. The only mark against it is finishing on $196: four dollars is a startable player in this format.

Verdict: the best player in the draft with a real plan B behind him. Quietly one of the sturdiest teams here.

Super Nintendo Chalmers

Cornered the receiver market, then had four of them go on bye together.

On WRs$162
PriciestCollins $32
Best buyMontgomery $18
Worst byeW8, 4 out

Eight receivers, $162 of $200, nobody costing more than $32. In a league starting four plus a flex, that is the position to corner, and this is a rotation rather than a lineup: Nico Collins, Chris Olave, Emeka Egbuka, Mike Evans, Jameson Williams, with Christian Watson and Davante Adams on the bench. David Montgomery for $18 against a $34 board was the single sharpest buy of the draft.

Week 8 is the bill: Montgomery, Collins, Olave and Evans all out together. Any other roster here would be sunk by that; this one just plays the next four receivers and barely notices, which is the whole point of buying a position in bulk. Goff throwing to Jameson Williams is a free stack on top.

Verdict: won the auction on value and built the only roster that can absorb its own worst week. Somebody should be calling about a receiver.

Everything’s Fine

Five starters share a bye. Five.

On WRs$144
PriciestJSN $48
RB2Tracy $1
Worst byeW11, 5 out

Jaxon Smith-Njigba, A.J. Brown and Tee Higgins for $106 is the best receiving trio anybody assembled, and in a four-receiver league that is exactly the right place to put the money. The top of this roster is not the issue.

The issue is Week 11, when Stafford, Kyren Williams, Smith-Njigba, A.J. Brown and Harold Fannin are all on bye — five of the nine starting spots, gone, in the same week, and that week sits right at the start of the fantasy playoff run-in. Behind Kyren at $41 the backfield is Tyrone Tracy at $1 with no handcuff, so the depth to paper over it is not there either. This is a fixable problem, but it needs fixing in October, not in Week 11.

Verdict: elite where this league pays best, with one week on the calendar that could cost the season.

Closed For Repairs

No stars, no scrubs, no bad weeks.

PriciestMcBride $34
At $1–23 players
BenchDobbins, Hubbard, Dowdle
Byesnever 3 out

Only three players cost a dollar or two — the rest of the league averages seven — and nobody cost more than Trey McBride at $34. Sixteen players who all cost real money. It is the least exciting draft here and quite possibly the most competent one.

The bench is the reason: J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle, all sitting there, any of whom would start for half this league. Combine that with byes that never take out more than two starters at once and you have the only roster in the league that genuinely does not care what happens to it. It will not blow anybody out. It will also never lose a week before kickoff, which over seventeen weeks is worth more than it sounds.

Verdict: boring in September, extremely difficult to beat in November.

Pleasantly Pathetic

Josh Allen, two good backs, and four wide receivers total.

Top three$147
WRs rostered4
HandcuffMitchell behind Hampton
Worst byeW7, 4 out

Josh Allen for $34, James Cook for $60, Omarion Hampton for $47. Three good players and a coherent theory — Allen is the one quarterback genuinely worth paying for here, and Cook is his teammate, so Buffalo scoring is worth double. They also took Keaton Mitchell behind Hampton in Los Angeles, which is proper preparation.

Then the arithmetic. $43 for thirteen roster spots, and exactly four wide receivers in a league that starts four — no flex option, no bye cover, nothing. Week 7 takes out Allen, Cook, Hampton and Antonio Williams together, which is most of the team. And one of the sixteen picks went on a defensive back, a position this league has no slot for: a dollar, and the funniest dollar of the night.

Verdict: the best player in the draft and no room whatsoever. Needs receivers more than anyone here.

“Columbus” Auction League · 2026

The Draft Recap

Twelve managers. $2,400. 192 players. And a room that flatly refused to pay for quarterbacks.

Everybody spent nearly everything — $2,393 of $2,400, with only three teams leaving so much as a dollar on the table. Jahmyr Gibbs was the priciest man in the room at $68. The cheapest starting quarterback cost $1. Four different teams paid that.

The quarterback thing

This league awards six points per passing touchdown, which is supposed to make quarterbacks expensive. Reader, it did not.

Patrick Mahomes went for $1. Trevor Lawrence, $1. Jared Goff, Matthew Stafford, Jordan Love, Kyler Murray, Brock Purdy — all $1. Dak Prescott cost $3. Justin Herbert, $4. Nine of the twelve teams walked away with a starting quarterback for eleven dollars or less, and several of them are now sitting on a backup who projects within ten points of their starter.

Josh Allen cost $34, and he may well be worth it. But the gap between him and a dollar bin full of 330-point arms is the single loudest thing in this draft — and the reason so many rosters have $30 more receiver than they probably expected.

Superlatives

The opening run. The first four players nominated were all running backs — Bijan Robinson ($64), Jahmyr Gibbs ($68), Jonathan Taylor ($66) and Christian McCaffrey ($53). $251 left the room in four nominations, before anybody had drafted a receiver.
Nearly half this draft cost pocket change. Sixty-one players went for $1 and another twenty for $2 — 81 of 192 roster spots, or 42% of the league, filled for a combined $101.
Steal of the draft. David Montgomery, $18 against a $34 board — Super Nintendo Chalmers. Honourable mention to Mike Evans at $16 and Brock Purdy at $1.
Biggest overpay, joint. Josh Jacobs at $15 against a $1 board (The Empire Strikes Dak) and DJ Moore at $23 against $9 (Washington Fantasy Team). Both fourteen dollars clear of what this room usually pays.
Most expensive man in the room. Jahmyr Gibbs, $68, The DC Lumber Company. Jonathan Taylor was next at $66.
Best dollar anybody spent. Brock Purdy, $1, Washington Fantasy Team. He projects 350 points against Josh Allen’s 406 — so the most expensive quarterback in the draft bought 56 extra points for $33.
Best bench player nobody can start. Patrick Mahomes, $1, sitting behind Lamar Jackson on The Empire Strikes Dak. Trevor Lawrence at $1 on Threat Level Midnight is right there with him.
Deepest bench that can actually play. Closed For Repairs, whose three best reserves — J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle — project 383 points between them. Only one other team clears 350; five are under 250.
Most committed to a bit. Super Nintendo Chalmers, who put $162 of $200 into wide receivers and then watched it work.
Most expensive four dollars nobody spent. The DC Lumber Company finished on $196. In an auction where a dollar was buying 330-point quarterbacks, that is real money left in the wallet.
Last man off the board. Joe Mixon, $1, pick 192, to Pleasantly Pathetic — who also took pick 190. A quiet, dignified end to proceedings.

Team by team

Roughly in order of projected starting lineup — though the middle of that order is separated by about a point and a half a week, less than the error on anybody's projections, so read it as a running order rather than a ranking. East Village All-Stars

The best starting eleven in the league, and nine wide receivers.

Spent$199
Best lineup1680
Cover328
vs board+23

The strongest lineup in the league, and it is built on efficiency rather than on any one player. Nothing on this roster came in more than four dollars over its board price, Bijan Robinson at $64 landed almost exactly at market, and Sam LaPorta for $4 and Jadarian Price for $10 were both proper steals. Plus 23 against the board is second-best in the room.

The oddity is nine wide receivers on a roster that starts four and a flex. It looks like the deepest bench in the league — and by raw points it is, comfortably — but a good chunk of that total is a backup quarterback who cannot get on the field. Count only players who could actually start and the cover is 328: good, fourth-best, not the runaway it appears. There is very likely a trade in here that turns two of those receivers into an upgrade at running back, where the roster is four deep and leaning hard on Bijan.

Verdict: the strongest lineup in the draft, bought at a discount. Now go and consolidate some of it.

The most balanced draft in the room. Nobody cost more than $49, the money splits almost exactly down the middle between running backs ($91) and receivers ($93), and the result is the second-strongest lineup in the league, with the second-best cover behind it. This is what refusing to fall in love with anybody looks like.

The comedy is at quarterback. Caleb Williams for $5, then Trevor Lawrence for $1 — two passers projecting within two points of each other. Six dollars for a starter and his identical twin, who now watches from the bench. Colston Loveland at $9 against a $17 board was the tidiest buy on the roster.

Verdict: no weaknesses, no drama, no obvious way to attack it. Extremely annoying.

The most concentrated roster in the league by a distance. Derrick Henry ($53), Christian McCaffrey ($53) and De'Von Achane ($44) take $150 of the $200, leaving $43 for four starting receivers and ten players at a dollar or two.

When it works it is glorious. But the cover is 244, third-worst in the league, and the weakest man in the starting lineup is a receiver projecting 74 points. This is a roster with no answers, only starters — and it is asking three running backs with a lot of career mileage to all stay upright at once. Verdict: a beautiful, terrifying machine. Two hamstrings from a rebuild.

Brock Bowers at $33 and Tyler Warren at $8 — $41 at tight end, nearly triple the league average, and the only roster that starts two of them by putting one in the flex. It is a real edge: the median starting tight end here is worth about a hundred points, so having two good ones beats half the league at that slot every week.

It is paid for with a lean backfield ($61, nobody over $31) and Puka Nacua at $51 holding the receivers together. Cover is 222, second-lowest in the league. The strategy is sound; the margin for error is not.

Verdict: the most distinctive roster in the draft. If the tight ends hit, nobody has an answer.

The Empire Strikes Dak

Two elite quarterbacks for twelve dollars, and a very expensive backfield.

Spent$198
Best lineup1593
Cover276
RB spend$113

The quarterback room is the quiet coup of the whole draft. Lamar Jackson at $11 was one of the best buys of the night against a $21 board, and then Patrick Mahomes arrived for a dollar — a genuine top-five arm who will spend most Sundays on the bench. Twelve dollars for that pair, in a six-point-passing-touchdown league, is close to daylight robbery.

The backfield is where the money went and where the risk lives. Jonathan Taylor at $66 was the second-priciest player in the draft and about ten over the board; Josh Jacobs at $15 was the single largest overpay in the room. $113 in running backs is a lot of eggs in a basket that gets tackled for a living — though Travis Kelce on the bench is a nice piece of insurance.

Verdict: paid up at the one position everybody else got for free, and still got the cheapest quarterbacks in the league.

Jahmyr Gibbs at $68 was the top price of the auction, and paired with Jeremiyah Love at $39 makes $129 of running back. The receivers that money left behind are thin — Tetairoa McMillan at $29 and then a sharp drop.

Verdict: the best player in the draft plus the depth to survive losing him. Better than it looks on paper.

The best value draft in the league, and it is not close. Plus 37 against the board, including the single biggest bargain of the night — David Montgomery at $18 against $34 — and Mike Evans at $16 against $30.

The shape is extreme: $162 of $200 on eight wide receivers, nobody costing more than $32, with a dollar quarterback and a dollar tight end. The starting lineup projects in the lower half today, but the cover is 380 — second-best in the league, with Christian Watson and Davante Adams waiting on the bench. Depth at receiver in a four-receiver league is not a luxury, it is a weekly advantage.

Verdict: won the auction on points per dollar. Expect this one to climb.

Only three players on this roster cost a dollar or two — by far the fewest in a league averaging seven — and nobody cost more than Trey McBride at $34. Sixteen players who all cost something, and a lineup with no obvious hole and no obvious weapon.

Which is why the lineup number is misleading. The cover is the best in the league at 383: J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle are all sitting on the bench projecting over 120 apiece. The weakest starting lineup on paper today, and the only roster here that barely notices when somebody gets hurt. McBride at $34 against a $21 board was the one splurge; Drake Maye at $11 quietly paid for most of it.

Verdict: the projection undersells this badly. Built for November, not for September.

Pleasantly Pathetic

The best player in football, and then the arithmetic ran out.

Spent$200
Best lineup1505
Cover218
WRs rostered4

Josh Allen ($34), James Cook ($60) and Omarion Hampton ($47) is $147 on three players, and all three are good. Allen is the highest-projecting quarterback in the league by a wide margin, and in a six-point-passing-touchdown format that is a permanent weekly edge.

The problem is what was left: $43 for everything else, and exactly four wide receivers in a league that starts four. Two of them cost a dollar. There is no flex option, no bye-week cover, and the lowest cover number in the league at 218. One of the sixteen roster spots also went to a DB, in a league with nowhere to play one — which, at a dollar, is the cheapest mistake anybody made all night.

Verdict: the best single player in the draft and the least room to manoeuvre. It all rests on Allen.

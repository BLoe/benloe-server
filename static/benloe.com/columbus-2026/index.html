“Columbus” Auction League · 2026

The Draft Recap

Twelve managers. $2,400. 192 players. And a room that flatly refused to pay for quarterbacks.

Everybody spent nearly everything — $2,393 of $2,400, with only three teams leaving so much as a dollar on the table. Jahmyr Gibbs was the priciest man in the room at $68. The cheapest starting quarterback cost $1. Four different teams paid that.

The quarterback thing

This league awards six points per passing touchdown, which is supposed to make quarterbacks expensive. Reader, it did not.

Patrick Mahomes went for $1. Trevor Lawrence, $1. Jared Goff, Matthew Stafford, Jordan Love, Kyler Murray, Brock Purdy — all $1. Dak Prescott cost $3. Justin Herbert, $4. Nine of the twelve teams walked away with a starting quarterback for eleven dollars or less, and several of them are now sitting on a backup who projects within ten points of their starter.

Josh Allen cost $34, and he may well be worth it. But the gap between him and a dollar bin full of 330-point arms is the single loudest thing in this draft — and the reason so many rosters have $30 more receiver than they probably expected.

Superlatives

The opening run. The first four players nominated were all running backs — Bijan Robinson ($64), Jahmyr Gibbs ($68), Jonathan Taylor ($66) and Christian McCaffrey ($53). $251 left the room in four nominations, before anybody had drafted a receiver.
Nearly half this draft cost pocket change. Sixty-one players went for $1 and another twenty for $2 — 81 of 192 roster spots, or 42% of the league, filled for a combined $101.
Steal of the draft. David Montgomery, $18 against a $34 board — Super Nintendo Chalmers. Honourable mention to Mike Evans at $16 and Brock Purdy at $1.
Biggest overpay, joint. Josh Jacobs at $15 against a $1 board (The Empire Strikes Dak) and DJ Moore at $23 against $9 (Washington Fantasy Team). Both fourteen dollars clear of what this room usually pays.
Most expensive man in the room. Jahmyr Gibbs, $68, The DC Lumber Company. Jonathan Taylor was next at $66.
Best dollar anybody spent. Brock Purdy, $1, Washington Fantasy Team. He projects 350 points against Josh Allen’s 406 — so the most expensive quarterback in the draft bought 56 extra points for $33.
Best bench player nobody can start. Patrick Mahomes, $1, sitting behind Lamar Jackson on The Empire Strikes Dak. Trevor Lawrence at $1 on Threat Level Midnight is right there with him.
Deepest bench that can actually play. Closed For Repairs, whose three best reserves — J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle — project 383 points between them. Only one other team clears 350; five are under 250.
Most committed to a bit. Super Nintendo Chalmers, who put $162 of $200 into wide receivers and then watched it work.
Most expensive four dollars nobody spent. The DC Lumber Company finished on $196. In an auction where a dollar was buying 330-point quarterbacks, that is real money left in the wallet.
Last man off the board. Joe Mixon, $1, pick 192, to Pleasantly Pathetic — who also took pick 190. A quiet, dignified end to proceedings.

Team by team

“The board” below means Gavel’s pre-draft price for a player, calculated from this league’s own 2024 and 2025 auction results — so a bargain here is a bargain against what this room actually pays, not against a magazine.

East Village All-Stars

Nine wide receivers. The league starts four.

Spent$199
PriciestBijan $64
WRs rostered9
Over the boardnever by more than $4

Opened the entire auction by nominating Bijan Robinson and paying $64 for him, which turned out to be almost exactly the right price, and then never overpaid for anybody again — not one player on this roster came in more than four dollars above what the board said he was worth. Sam LaPorta for $4 is the sort of thing that wins a season quietly. Jadarian Price for $10 is the sort of thing you brag about in March.

And then, somewhere around the fourth hour, this became a wide receiver collection. Nine of them. Amon-Ra St. Brown and DeVonta Smith start; Matthew Golden and Makai Lemon start; Quentin Johnston and Romeo Doubs sit on a bench they are unlikely to leave, because the only way onto this field is for someone ahead of them to get hurt. Meanwhile the running back room is Bijan and three lottery tickets. There is a trade in here — two receivers for one back — and it is worth making before somebody else notices.

Verdict: the best starting eleven in the league, assembled at a discount, with a small warehouse of receivers attached. Go shopping.

Threat Level Midnight

Bought the same quarterback twice.

Spent$200
PriciestSaquon $49
RB / WR split$91 / $93
Best buyLoveland $9

The most level-headed draft in the room. Nobody cost more than Saquon Barkley at $49, the money went almost exactly half to running backs and half to receivers, and the roster that came out the other end has no hole anywhere. Breece Hall, Drake London, Ladd McConkey, Brian Thomas — none of them a headline, all of them starters. Colston Loveland at $9 was the pickpocketing highlight.

The one genuinely funny moment: Caleb Williams for $5, and then, a while later, Trevor Lawrence for $1. Two quarterbacks who project within two points of one another. Six dollars for a starter and a spare part that is exactly the same shape as the starter. Somewhere behind that bid is a manager who forgot he had already solved this.

Verdict: nothing to attack, nothing to laugh at, one redundant quarterback. Extremely irritating.

Washington Fantasy Team

Three running backs and a very strong opinion.

Spent$200
On RBs$153
On WRs$43
Players at $1–210

Derrick Henry, Christian McCaffrey and De'Von Achane. $150 of the $200, gone, on three men who all do the same job. It is a real plan and on the right Sunday it will bury people — but it left $43 for four starting receiver spots, and the fourth of those is currently Ryan Flournoy, acquired with the second-to-last pick of the entire draft.

The bench is Rachaad White and a prayer. If any one of the big three misses a month, there is nothing behind them but dollar players, and this roster goes from frightening to ordinary in a single afternoon. On the other hand: Brock Purdy for $1, which is the best individual dollar anybody spent all night.

Verdict: a beautiful, terrifying machine with no spare parts. Draft insurance early.

Ten of the sixteen players here cost a dollar or two, and yet the starting lineup is genuinely good: Justin Jefferson, Zay Flowers, Garrett Wilson and Terry McLaurin is a receiving corps most of this league would take, and Kenneth Walker at $40 was four dollars under what the board said. This is what happens when you refuse to bid on anything until everyone else has spent.

The quarterback cost a dollar. The tight end cost a dollar. That is fine — right up until the week one of them has a bye, at which point the plan is Aaron Jones and hope. There is no slack anywhere on this roster, and the waiver wire is about to become a part-time job.

Verdict: far better than the price tags suggest. Set an alarm for bye weeks.

On TEs$41
PriciestNacua $51
League average TE spend$14

Brock Bowers for $33, and then Tyler Warren for $8 — the biggest tight end bill in the league, in a room where seven of the twelve teams spent five dollars or less there, and the only roster that plays two of them by shoving one into the flex. It is not a mistake, it is a weapon: half this league is starting someone who projects around a hundred points there, and this team has two who beat that comfortably.

It was paid for out of the backfield, which is Javonte Williams, Quinshon Judkins and hope, and out of the bottom of the receiving corps, where Jalen Nailor is currently a starter. The bench behind all this is Denzel Boston and Tre' Harris. Everything has to stay upright.

Verdict: the most interesting roster in the draft. If the tight ends hit, nobody has an answer for it.

IslandExclusives

Two starting quarterbacks, one sandwich.

Spent$200
PriciestChase $55
QBsBurrow $19 + Dak $3
Best bench playerMark Andrews

Ja'Marr Chase for $55 anchors it, and the quarterback room is the quiet triumph of the whole night: Joe Burrow for $19, then Dak Prescott for $3 — two starters, ten points apart across a whole season, for the price of a round of drinks. That is a matchup lever nobody else in this league has.

The wobble was Ashton Jeanty at $49, twelve dollars clear of the board and a lot of faith in a rookie running back. It is largely forgiven by George Kittle for $2 and DK Metcalf for $3, and by Mark Andrews sitting on the bench as insurance at a position where most of this league has none.

Verdict: bargain hunting of a very high order, either side of one enthusiastic bid.

The Empire Strikes Dak

Patrick Mahomes: one dollar.

Spent$198
PriciestTaylor $66
QBsLamar $11 + Mahomes $1
On RBs$113

Lamar Jackson for $11 was one of the best buys of the draft. Then, later, with nobody paying attention, Patrick Mahomes for a dollar. In a league that pays six points a passing touchdown, this roster acquired two genuine franchise quarterbacks for twelve dollars total, and one of them will spend the season watching.

The rest of the money went into the backfield — $113, headlined by Jonathan Taylor at $66, the second-priciest player of the night and about ten dollars rich. The Josh Jacobs bid at $15 was the most enthusiastic overpay in the room against a board price of one dollar. Travis Kelce on the bench is a nice place to land, though.

Verdict: paid a premium at the one position everyone else got for free, and still won the quarterback market.

The DC Lumber Company

Bought the most expensive man in the draft, then quietly built the best bench.

Spent$196
PriciestGibbs $68
Left unspent$4
Bench RBsBrooks, Corum

Jahmyr Gibbs at $68 was the top price of the auction, and Jeremiyah Love at $39 put another big number next to it. That is a lot of running back, and it shows in the receiving corps, where the drop after Tetairoa McMillan is steep enough to be audible.

What makes this roster better than it looks is the back half of it. Jonathon Brooks and Blake Corum are both sitting on the bench and both would start for several teams in this league — this is one of very few rosters here that can lose a starter and shrug. The only real sin is finishing on $196: four dollars left unspent in an auction where a dollar was buying 330-point quarterbacks.

Verdict: the best player in the draft and an actual plan B. Better than it looks.

Super Nintendo Chalmers

Bought every receiver in the building and kept the change.

Spent$200
On WRs$162
PriciestNico Collins $32
Best buyMontgomery $18

Eight wide receivers, $162 of the $200, and not one player on the roster costing more than $32. It should not work and it absolutely does: Nico Collins, Chris Olave, Emeka Egbuka, Mike Evans and Jameson Williams is a rotation, not a starting lineup, in a league that starts four of them plus a flex.

This was also the shrewdest draft in the room. David Montgomery for $18 when the board said $34 was the single biggest bargain of the night; Mike Evans at $16 was not far behind. Christian Watson and Davante Adams are on the bench. The quarterback and the tight end cost a dollar each and it barely matters.

Verdict: won the auction on value by a mile and has receivers to trade. Somebody will regret ignoring this.

Everything’s Fine

Three brilliant receivers and a one-dollar running back.

Spent$200
On WRs$144
RB2Tyrone Tracy $1
PriciestJSN $48

Jaxon Smith-Njigba, A.J. Brown and Tee Higgins for $106 between them is the best receiving trio anybody assembled, and in a four-receiver league that is exactly where you want your money. No complaints at all about the top of this roster.

It is the bottom that raises an eyebrow. Behind Kyren Williams at $41, the running back situation is Tyrone Tracy at $1, and the bench behind him is Tyjae Spears. The whole season is riding on one hamstring, and everyone can see which one.

Verdict: elite where this league pays best, and one bad Sunday from an interesting autumn.

Closed For Repairs

Nobody cheap, nobody expensive, everybody useful.

Spent$200
PriciestMcBride $34
Players at $1–23
BenchDobbins, Hubbard, Dowdle

Only three players on this roster cost a dollar or two — the rest of the league averages seven — and nobody cost more than Trey McBride at $34. Sixteen players who all cost real money, no stars, no scrubs, no drama. It is the least exciting draft here and possibly the most sensible.

The payoff is the bench, which is absurd: J.K. Dobbins, Chuba Hubbard and Rico Dowdle, all sitting there, any of whom would start for half this league. Nobody else is anywhere close to that. This roster will not win many weeks outright, but it is the only one that genuinely does not care when somebody gets hurt — and by week ten, that tends to be the same thing.

Verdict: boring in September, quietly terrifying in November.

Pleasantly Pathetic

Josh Allen, two very good running backs, and then the money ran out.

Spent$200
Top three$147
WRs rostered4
Also drafteda DB

Josh Allen for $34, James Cook for $60, Omarion Hampton for $47. Three excellent players, $147, and a completely defensible theory: Allen is the best quarterback in football and this league pays six a passing touchdown, so own him.

That left $43 for the other thirteen roster spots, and the receiving corps is exactly four men in a league that starts four. Two of them cost a dollar. There is no flex, no bye cover, and no margin whatsoever. And in the interests of full disclosure, one of the sixteen picks was spent on a defensive back, a position this league does not have a slot for — a dollar, and the funniest dollar of the night.

Verdict: the best single player in the draft and the least room to move. It all rests on Allen.
